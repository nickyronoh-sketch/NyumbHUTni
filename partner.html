PROJECT 01

Last-Mile SATO Toilet Installation Project – Bomet County

Project Period: November 2026 – November 2028

Bringing Improved Sanitation Closer to Every Household

Access to safe and dignified sanitation remains a critical development priority for many households. Inadequate sanitation exposes communities to preventable diseases, environmental contamination and poor living conditions.

NyumbHUTni is spearheading a community-focused initiative aimed at increasing access to improved household sanitation through the last-mile distribution and installation of SATO toilet solutions.

The project seeks to facilitate the installation of SATO toilets in households, particularly in communities where access to improved sanitation facilities remains a challenge.

By bringing affordable and appropriate sanitation technologies closer to households, we aim to contribute to improved hygiene practices, reduced exposure to faecal contamination and a reduction in faecal-oral disease transmission.

How the project works

The initiative adopts a community-driven implementation model that brings together local institutions, community networks and development partners to facilitate:

  • Identification and mobilization of households requiring improved sanitation facilities.
  • Last-mile distribution and installation of SATO toilet products.
  • Community sensitization on improved sanitation and hygiene practices.
  • Engagement of local cooperatives, community groups and institutions in project implementation.
  • Monitoring and documentation of household installations and project impact.

PROJECT 02

Filters for Trees Initiative – Safeguarding the Mau-Mara Ecosystem

The Mau Forest Complex is one of Kenya's most important water towers and ecological assets. Its contribution to biodiversity, river systems and livelihoods extends across the wider Mau-Mara landscape and connected ecosystems towards Lake Victoria and the Serengeti.

However, increasing pressure on forest resources, including the use of firewood for domestic water treatment, presents an opportunity to explore alternative approaches that support both household access to safe drinking water and environmental conservation.

NyumbHUTni's Filters for Trees Initiative is an environmental conservation programme designed to promote household water filtration as an alternative to traditional water treatment methods that depend on firewood.

The initiative seeks to encourage households to adopt appropriate water filtration technologies, potentially reducing the need to boil drinking water using firewood and, consequently, helping reduce pressure on surrounding tree resources.

Through this initiative, we aim to connect access to safe drinking water with forest conservation, climate action and sustainable community livelihoods.

The initiative focuses on

  • Promoting household water filtration technologies as an alternative to firewood-dependent water treatment.
  • Supporting adoption of appropriate water purification solutions in communities around the Mau-Mara landscape.
  • Creating awareness on the relationship between household energy use, deforestation and environmental conservation.
  • Building partnerships that connect water access, forest protection and climate action.
  • Exploring measurable environmental outcomes, including potential reductions in household firewood consumption.
  • Developing opportunities for carbon finance and other innovative conservation financing mechanisms, subject to appropriate measurement and verification.

Geographical focus

The initiative seeks to engage communities and conservation stakeholders across the wider Mau-Mara ecosystem and its connected landscapes, with an initial geographical focus on:

  • Nakuru County
  • Narok County
  • Bomet County
  • The wider Mara ecosystem extending towards the Serengeti in Tanzania
